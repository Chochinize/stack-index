Obsidian keeps your entire knowledge base as a folder of plain **Markdown** files on your own disk. There's no server, no mandatory account, and nothing to sign into — open the app and your notes are just there, even offline on a plane.

Because the vault is plain text, your notes outlive the app: you can back them up, sync them with any tool, edit them in another editor, or grep them from a terminal. Bidirectional links and the graph view turn that folder into a connected web of ideas, and a large community plugin ecosystem extends it in every direction.

> Your data stays yours: local files first, optional paid end-to-end encrypted sync only if you want it.

**Good to know**

- Free for personal use; paid add-ons for official Sync and Publish.
- Not open source, but the data format (Markdown + attachments) is fully open and portable.
- Mobile apps read the same vault via your own sync (Syncthing, iCloud, Obsidian Sync…).
